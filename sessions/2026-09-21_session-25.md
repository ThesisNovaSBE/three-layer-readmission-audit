## Key decisions / findings

- **`generation_batch_size` VRAM ceiling found: works at 4, OOMs at 8 and
  12.** Both larger sizes failed with the identical CUDA OOM pattern
  (`Tried to allocate X GiB... only Y MiB free`) — genuine memory limits,
  not a code bug; `call_llm_batch`'s error handling caught both cleanly
  (all patients marked failed, job completed instead of crashing). At
  batch_size=8 there was only 547 MB free when ~1.9 GB more was needed —
  too tight a margin to trust a value between 4 and 8 without another
  full sweep cycle. Decision: stop sweeping and use `generation_batch_size=4`
  for the real run. Reasoning given to and accepted by the user: each sweep
  attempt costs a real queue-wait (one attempt alone took ~3 days given how
  congested the `kisski` partition got), the project's real deadline is
  January 2027 so multi-day compute isn't itself a problem, and the likely
  gain (maybe 1.5-2x, unproven) isn't worth more calendar time chasing.
- **The `kisski` partition got genuinely congested this session** — one job
  sat with a scheduler-predicted `StartTime` roughly 33 hours out
  (confirmed via `scontrol show job`, not guessed), caused by other groups'
  jobs, several explicitly out of their own core-hour budget
  (`AccountOutOfCoreH`) and blocking behind it. Unrelated to this project's
  account or code. User chose to just wait rather than pay `kisski-h100`'s
  much higher per-job billing rate.
- **User's call to run a 50-patient qualitative validation before the full
  run, and to read individual patients' reasoning myself rather than only
  trust aggregate stats, caught three real, previously-invisible bugs** —
  none of which the earlier 8-patient clean run had enough data to surface:
  1. **`verify_quote()` was structurally broken, not just imperfect.**
     `all_quotes_verified` was 0% across all 50 patients. Diagnosed by
     pulling the real MIMIC note text for this validation run's patients
     and checking a whitespace-normalized match against the same quotes:
     140 of 161 failing quotes (87%) were recovered. Root cause: MIMIC-IV
     notes hard-wrap at a fixed column width with literal `\n` characters
     mid-sentence; the model correctly reproduces quotes as flowing text,
     but the exact-substring check treated that formatting difference as a
     content mismatch. This wasn't a minor edge case — it meant the
     project's stated anti-hallucination guarantee ("a reviewer only needs
     to check the subset where a quote is unverified") was non-functional:
     every row looked equally suspect regardless of whether the model was
     faithfully quoting or fabricating. Fixed by normalizing whitespace on
     both sides of the comparison (collapsing all whitespace runs to a
     single space) — confirmed this doesn't weaken the actual
     anti-hallucination check (added a test with a genuinely fabricated
     quote that must still fail).
  2. **`strong_discharge_support` over-triggering, confirmed with real
     numbers, not just a hunch from the 8-patient sample.** Fired 62 times
     across 50 patients — the single most common ground, more than once
     per patient on average. Read through the actual cited quotes: several
     were pure boilerplate ("We wish you the best in your recovery," a
     generic "follow up with your doctors" instruction, a bare
     administrative field) that don't satisfy the ground's own stated
     three-part definition (follow-up arranged AND caregiver present AND
     clinically stable). At least one override decision (hadm_id 20019694)
     leaned on exactly this kind of weak evidence. Fixed by rewriting the
     ground's description to explicitly require all three conditions with
     concrete evidence and explicitly exclude the boilerplate patterns
     observed, plus a new general instruction in the prompt (applies to
     all grounds, not just this one) that generic pleasantries and routine
     non-specific instructions don't count as evidence on their own.
  3. **`clinical_justification`'s `maxLength=800` schema constraint cuts
     generation off at exactly 800 characters regardless of word/sentence
     boundaries** — several justifications ended mid-word ("...renal
     faili", "...encephal"), one with a stray non-ASCII character right at
     the cut point. Doesn't cause `annotation_failed` (the JSON is still
     valid), but reads as broken if shown anywhere. Fixed with a
     post-parse cleanup step, not a generation-time change: trims the
     justification back to the end of its last complete sentence, falling
     back to the raw text if no sentence boundary exists at all.
- **Overall verdict on whether the auditor's reasoning is worth trusting at
  scale: yes, with the above three fixes.** Read all 50 patients' full
  reasoning individually, not just the aggregate stats. The
  `decision_model` vs. `decision_rule` 86% agreement rate is exactly the
  documented, expected pattern (verified directly: every one of the 4
  cases where `decision_rule` said "override" had zero aggravating grounds
  cited, matching its documented all-or-nothing logic exactly). The
  hospice/palliative-care overrides and the "benign, resolved presentation"
  override read as genuinely sound clinical reasoning, not
  rubber-stamping. Decision distribution (78% uphold / 22% override) isn't
  degenerate. The three bugs above were real but fixable without
  redesigning the taxonomy or the overall approach.
