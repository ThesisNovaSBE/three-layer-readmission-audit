## What I worked on

Built a reproducible, **privacy-safe exploratory data analysis (EDA)** of
MIMIC-IV 3.1 (structured) and MIMIC-IV-Note 2.2 (discharge notes) as the
pipeline actually uses them, plus notebooks for continuing the EDA by hand.

The project had no EDA anywhere. Every number in the thesis so far comes
from model evaluation. The data chapter ("who is in the cohort, what does
the label mean, what does the data look like") had no single source, and
several modelling choices (patient-level split, capacity-K, 4096-token
window, Stage 3 grounds) rest on data properties nobody had measured
directly.

## Why each part exists (the reasoning)

The guiding question for choosing what to look at was: *what would an
examiner ask about the data that our current results cannot answer?*

| EDA section | Why it matters for this thesis |
|---|---|
| **Cohort flow** (CONSORT-style) | Standard first figure of any clinical-ML data chapter; verifies the EDA cohort is *identical* to the model cohort (521,191). |
| **Outcome / label audit** | The target is our own construction (unplanned = all-cause minus elective/same-day-surgical returns). We need to know what's *in* it: observation stays, same-day returns, overlapping admissions, and deaths after discharge (labelled negative = competing risk). |
| **Utilisation** (admissions per patient) | Justifies the patient-level split empirically, and shows where the readmissions concentrate. |
| **Table 1 + SMD** | The standard cohort-description table, split by outcome. SMD (Austin 2009) is the same balance metric `scripts/analyze_selection_bias.py` already uses. |
| **Subgroup base rates** | The fairness analysis (70+ gap) needs base rates per subgroup to separate "harder to predict" from "different prevalence". |
| **Temporal drift** | MIMIC spans 2008–2022, with the ICD-9→10 switch in 2015 (Charlson is computed from both) and COVID. Also where the note coverage drops (see findings). |
| **Note coverage** | Stage 2/3 run only on admissions *with* a discharge note, so who has one decides whom RQ1/RQ2 generalise to. |
| **Missingness** (overall, by ICU, by outcome) | XGBoost routes NaN natively, so missingness becomes a *feature*. We need to know what it encodes. `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` listed "vitals missingness" as an open feature-audit item. |
| **Univariate AUROC + redundancy** | A cheap, model-free view of where the signal lives; a sanity check against SHAP. |
| **Measurement quality** (per itemid) | The curated itemid map in `src/data/features.py` says "adjust against d_labitems if needed" and had never been audited. Checks units, pre-admission/post-discharge values, and how stale the "last" value is. |
| **Diagnoses** | ICD-9/10 mix, Charlson distribution, readmission rate by primary-diagnosis chapter. |
| **Notes: length / truncation** | Stage 2 reads only the first 4096 tokens: how many notes are cut, and *which sections* are cut? |
| **Notes: sections + placeholder-only sections** | Stage 3's grounds (`lives_alone_no_support`, `no_followup_arranged`, …) assume the information is in the note. De-identification may have removed it. |
| **Notes: Stage 3 concept keywords** | How common the grounds' vocabulary is, and whether it shifts the base rate (lexical proxy only; no negation handling). |
| **Notes: timing** | Is the note available at discharge time? This is deployment realism for a discharge-time tool. |

## Privacy design (key decision)

MIMIC is under the PhysioNet credentialed DUA: aggregate results may be
published, but nothing that could re-identify a patient or redistribute the
data. Rules, enforced in code (`src/eda/privacy.py`) and in tests
(`tests/test_eda_privacy.py`):

1. **Aggregates only.** No IDs, dates, rows or note text in any output;
   `write_json` refuses any output containing `subject_id`, `hadm_id`,
   `note_id`, `stay_id`, `text` or a raw DataFrame.
2. **Small-cell suppression at < 11** (CMS cell-size policy). Counts become
   `"<11"`, and a rate with a small numerator *or* denominator becomes
   `null`. Categories are folded into "Other" rather than masked, so totals
   stay honest.
3. **No extremes.** Distributions are reported as p1–p99 quantiles with no
   min/max, and histograms are clipped to [p1, p99] with bins under 11
   zeroed. Figures are drawn *only* from these aggregates.
4. **No free text.** Notes are streamed in chunks and reduced to per-note
   numbers/flags; the text is discarded per chunk. No excerpts or
   keyword-in-context snippets.
5. **Notebooks committed without outputs** (enforced by a test). The
   row-level cache lives in `data/interim/eda_cache/` (gitignored).

Other decisions:

- **Rebuild the feature matrix from raw rather than read
  `data/processed/features.csv`.** The local copy (11 Aug) is **stale**: it
  predates `readmission_30d_unplanned`, `has_discharge_note`,
  `admission_type_observation` and `is_medicaid_medicare`. The EDA calls the
  pipeline's own `build_features` and caches the result separately, so it
  never overwrites the pipeline's file. It asserts that its cohort equals
  the pipeline's cohort.
- **Own loader that keeps `itemid`.** The pipeline's loader drops it, and
  without it the unit audit below isn't possible.
- **Token counts use the real Stage 2 tokenizer** (`models/clinical_longformer`),
  with `max_seq_length` taken from `config.yaml`, so "truncated" means
  exactly what Stage 2 saw.

## Key findings

See `results/eda/README.md` for the full write-up with numbers and figures.
The ones that touch existing claims or code:

1. **~43% of "unplanned readmissions" (the model target) are observation
   stays.** That is 40.2% of all-cause returns: EU OBSERVATION 18.0%,
   OBSERVATION ADMIT 17.4%, DIRECT/AMBULATORY OBS 4.8%.
   Observation stays are outpatient status in US billing and do **not**
   count as readmissions under CMS HRRP. Our target is therefore broader
   than the policy definition the introduction motivates with.
2. **The six vital-sign features are 100% missing for non-ICU admissions**
   (86% overall). For XGBoost they act as a hidden "had ICU stay" flag, and
   where present they're stale: the last vital is a median **76 h before
   discharge** (ICU values from before the ward phase).
3. **`temperature_last` mixes °F and °C.** itemid 223761 (°F, median 98.4)
   and 223762 (°C, median 37.0, 6.6k admissions) map to the same feature.
   This is a real bug in `VITAL_ITEMIDS`; the impact is likely small (a tree
   can split on it), but it's semantically wrong.
4. **Only 8% of 2020–2022 admissions have a discharge note** (vs ~70% in
   2008–2019). MIMIC-IV-Note 2.2 doesn't cover the most recent years, so the
   Stage 2/3 population is temporally skewed and excludes the COVID era
   almost entirely. EU OBSERVATION admissions also have low coverage (27%).
5. **Competing risk:** 8,304 admissions (2.0% of negatives) died within 30
   days of discharge without readmission and are labelled "not readmitted".
6. **13.3% of readmissions happen within 1 day of discharge**: candidates
   for transfers or administrative re-registration rather than clinical
   readmissions. 47 index admissions overlap with the next one.
7. **Readmissions concentrate in frequent users.** Patients with 11+
   admissions supply 18.6% of admissions but 37.5% of readmissions.
   Single-admission patients (23% of admissions) are almost never positive
   (0.6%), which is structural: a positive needs a next admission.
8. **37% of curated lab values have no `hadm_id`** (outpatient labs) and
   are silently dropped by the pipeline.
9. **Truncation is outcome- and age-correlated.** 27.9% of notes exceed
   4,096 tokens: 36.0% of readmitted vs 26.0% of non-readmitted, and 18.1%
   (18–40) rising to 32.5% (70+). This is an untested candidate partial
   explanation for the 70+ discrimination gap.
10. **Truncation cuts the discharge sections.** Of notes with each section,
    19.8% lose Discharge Disposition, 22.0% Discharge Instructions and
    27.8% Followup Instructions, while the history sections are ~never cut.
11. **De-identification blanked the social context.** Social History is
    `___`-only in 97.1% of notes and Followup Instructions in 100%; "lives
    alone" appears in 0.8% of notes. Stage 3's `lives_alone_no_support` /
    `no_followup_arranged` grounds therefore have almost no textual basis
    in this dataset.
12. Notes are stored a median 3.4 h after discharge, ~29% more than 24 h
    after: a real discharge-time tool would often not have the final note.

## What's broken / open problems

- Findings 1–4 are **not fixed**. They're reported here for a team decision.
  Fixing 3 (the temperature unit) or changing 1 (the label) would require
  retraining Stage 1 → 2 → 3, which is out of scope this close to writing.
  The realistic path is to **disclose them as limitations** (MODEL_CARD.md
  "Limitations") and, at most, run a sensitivity analysis.
- The keyword concepts are lexical proxies (no negation handling, so "denies
  alcohol use" counts as a mention). Fine for prevalence, not for clinical
  claims.
- The real-year mapping (`admission_year_group`) is approximate to the
  3-year anchor window.
